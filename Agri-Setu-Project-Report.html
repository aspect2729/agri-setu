Smart India Hackathon — Project Report

Agri Setu

Organizing the agricultural market around the farmer — a White Store network for graded produce, instant payment, and QR-verified bulk trade.

Prototype: full-stack web application
Domain: AgriTech / supply-chain
Stack: Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS 4, Supabase
Date: 2 September 2026

1. Abstract

Small and marginal farmers in India rarely sell directly to bulk buyers. Supply is fragmented into small lots, quality is unverifiable, payment is delayed, and a chain of intermediaries captures the margin. Agri Setu is a working prototype of a different market structure: farmers bring produce to a nearby White Store collection centre, where it is weighed, quality-graded, and paid for immediately. Each accepted lot becomes a QR-coded batch. Bulk buyers browse ranked supply, order directly, and verify origin on delivery. Logistics partners move graded crates from store to warehouse. An admin console matches open demand against aggregated store inventory.

The system is implemented as six role-specific portals (farmer, store employee, store manager, buyer, logistics, admin) plus a public batch-trace page. It is a Smart India Hackathon prototype: real authentication and Postgres data, simulated cash, haversine distance instead of a maps SDK, and typed batch codes instead of live camera scanning.

2. Problem statement

India’s horticultural and food-grain markets are structurally unfavourable to the producer. A farmer with 20–50 kg of tomatoes cannot attract a mandi trader’s attention, let alone a processor or supermarket. Typical failure modes:

  • Fragmented supply. Small lots never reach a volume that bulk buyers will contract for.
  • Unverifiable quality. Grade is asserted, not recorded. Buyers discount every load for risk.
  • Delayed payment. Farmers wait days or weeks; distress sales follow.
  • Opaque prices. Intermediaries know both ends of the market; the farmer sees only the last offer.
  • No traceability. A crate in a city warehouse cannot be tied back to a village, a grade, or a weighment.

The conventional response — “put the farmer on an app so they can find buyers” — still makes the weaker party search a distant market. Agri Setu inverts that: the market is organized around the farmer’s supply, at a collection point they can actually reach.

Don’t make the farmer search for the market. Organize the market around the farmer.

3. Proposed solution

A White Store is a village-level collection centre. It is not a mandi stall and not a full cold warehouse. It is a trusted counter: weigh, grade, pay, label, hold, and dispatch.

Farmer lists produce → nearest White Store suggested → weigh · grade · pay on the spot → QR batch → buyers browse & score → order → logistics pickup → buyer verifies QR → store cash ledger updates

Aggregation is the economic core. Farmer A has 20 kg, Farmer B 30 kg, Farmer C 50 kg. Alone, none of them is visible to a bulk buyer. Together at one White Store they are 100 kg of graded, priced, QR-verified supply.

3.1 Design principles

  • Pay the farmer first. Payment is recorded the moment produce is received, not after the buyer settles.
  • Grade at the gate. A / B / C quality is assigned by store staff and locked onto the batch.
  • One identity per lot. Every accepted submission gets a unique AGS-XXXXXX code and a public trace URL.
  • Score, don’t dump a catalogue. Buyers see a match score from price, quality, quantity, and distance.
  • Single-store fulfilment for a demand. Admin matching allocates from one White Store so logistics stays simple.

5. System architecture

Agri Setu is a Next.js App Router application. Role dashboards are server-rendered pages that load the signed-in profile, query Supabase, map rows into view models, and hydrate a client app. Mutations go through server actions in lib/actions.ts. A request proxy (proxy.ts) redirects unauthenticated visitors away from protected prefixes.

6. Key algorithms

6.1 Buyer match score

Listings are scored relative to the current marketplace, not against a global constant. Each component is scaled 0–100, then combined:

score = 0.35·price + 0.30·quality + 0.15·quantity + 0.20·location

  • Price — cheaper lots score higher (min–max normalisation across visible listings).
  • Quality — grade weights A = 1.00, B = 0.65, C = 0.35.
  • Quantity — available kg relative to the largest lot on the board.
  • Location — inverse of haversine distance from the buyer to the White Store.

The buyer UI surfaces the breakdown (price / quality / quantity / location) so the rank is explainable, not a black box.

6.2 Admin supply–demand matching

For an open demand, received batches of that crop with remaining stock are grouped by White Store. The store whose aggregated remaining kg covers the full demand, and whose total is largest, is selected. Allocation is FIFO across that store’s batches (oldest first, partial take from the last). An order, order items, demand status matched, remaining-kg decrements, and a delivery request are written in one flow. Single-store fulfilment is deliberate: one pickup for logistics.

6.3 Pricing

Farmer payout is the counter price entered at weighing. Buyer-facing price is that payout plus a 15% White Store margin (salePricePerKg). Demand matching may instead use the buyer’s offered price when present. Confirming receipt writes a cash-in entry of quantity × agreed price to the store ledger.

6.4 Delivery state machine

requested → accepted → picked_up → in_transit → delivered. Accept binds the logistics profile. Status updates also move the parent order (pickup_scheduled / in_transit / delivered) so the buyer tracking timeline stays consistent.

8. Demonstration scenario

Seeded accounts (password agrisetu123) leave the story mid-flight: 400 kg of graded tomatoes at Rampur White Store, Ramesh Kumar’s 100 kg waiting at the counter, a pending 100 kg onion order from FreshMart, and an open 500 kg tomato demand.

  1. Employee weighs Ramesh’s 100 kg, grades A at ₹20/kg — farmer paid ₹2,000, QR batch created.
  2. Farmer portal shows the payout and batch code.
  3. Employee accepts and dispatches FreshMart’s onion order (delivery request appears for logistics).
  4. Admin matches the 500 kg tomato demand against now-complete Rampur stock; a second delivery is created.
  5. Logistics accepts both loads and advances them to delivered.
  6. Buyer pastes a batch code to verify origin and confirms receipt.
  7. Store manager sees inventory, farmer records, and cash in/out update through the chain.

9. Impact

DimensionIf the model were adopted
Farmer income Immediate cash at the gate reduces distress sales. Small lots become sellable through aggregation.
Buyer trust Grade, weight, and origin are on a scanable record, not a verbal claim. Match scores make trade-offs explicit.
Local employment White Stores need counter staff, graders, and a logistics partner — village-level work, not only urban platforms.
Food loss Faster offtake of perishable lots (and a clearance surface for ageing stock) shortens time sitting ungraded.
Policy fit Aligns with FPO / collection-centre thinking: a physical node plus a digital ledger, rather than an app that bypasses the village entirely.

10. Novelty

Agri-marketplace apps typically list farms to buyers or run e-NAM-style auction screens. Agri Setu’s distinctive combination is:

  • a physical White Store as the unit of aggregation, not the individual farm listing;
  • instant farmer payment decoupled from buyer settlement;
  • grade locked to a QR batch that anyone can verify without an account;
  • an explainable match score instead of an unsorted catalogue;
  • closed-loop logistics and cash so dispatch and the store ledger move with the same order.

11. Future work

  • UPI / Aadhaar-linked farmer payouts and buyer escrow.
  • Camera QR scan in the buyer and logistics apps; GPS breadcrumbs on trips.
  • Cold-chain flags, crate-level humidity, and spoilage risk on the match score.
  • Multi-store pooling when no single centre holds the full demand.
  • Tighter RLS: farmers see own lots, buyers see marketplace batches, logistics see assigned trips.
  • Seasonal declarations feeding admin planning and MSP / procurement windows.
  • Offline-capable employee counter (poor connectivity at the gate).

12. Conclusion

Agri Setu is a complete, role-faithful prototype of a farmer-centric market: collection, grading, instant pay, traceable batches, scored buying, matching, logistics, and settlement recording. It does not claim to replace mandis overnight. It shows that if the market is organized at a White Store the farmer can walk to, 20 kg stops being invisible — and a bulk buyer can trust what arrives.

The codebase is structured for a live 3-minute demo with seeded accounts. What remains for production is payments, maps, scanning, and hardened authorization — not the product idea itself.
